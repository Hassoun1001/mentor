# Sources for the trading book

Claims are written in the book's own words. 'Not re-opened' marks a source
cited from general knowledge rather than checked during the research pass.

## Carry

- Brunnermeier, Nagel, Pedersen (2008). [Carry trades and currency crashes](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473): Carry returns are negatively skewed: slow gains and sudden crashes when funding dries up.
- Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen, Vrugt (2018). [Carry](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:127:y:2018:i:2:p:197-225): Carry predicts returns across asset classes and is not explained by value or momentum.
- Lustig, Roussanov, Verdelhan (2011). [Common risk factors in currency markets](https://repository.upenn.edu/fnce_papers/335): Carry returns reflect exposure to a common global risk factor.

## Execution

- Almgren, Chriss (2000). [Optimal execution of portfolio transactions](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04867v2): Trading a large order fast costs impact, slowly costs price risk. Linked page is a recent paper summarising the model.
- Perold (1988). [The implementation shortfall: paper versus reality](https://www.proquest.com/docview/195579087) (not re-opened): The paper that named this cost. Only the catalogue record was seen.

## Forecasting

- Rossi (2013). [Exchange rate predictability](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:jeclit:v:51:y:2013:i:4:p:1063-1119): Forecasting ability depends on predictor, horizon and sample; the random walk without drift is the toughest benchmark.
- Gu, Kelly, Xiu (2020). [Empirical asset pricing via machine learning](https://nber.org/papers/w25398): Trees and neural networks led among methods predicting stock returns; momentum, liquidity and volatility were the main predictors.
- Hamilton (1989). [Regime-switching models (overview by the author)](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jhamilton/palgrav1.pdf): Models where hidden states switch the behaviour of a time series.
- Mandelbrot; Engle (ARCH) (1963 / 1982). [Volatility clustering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatility_clustering): Large price changes tend to be followed by large changes, of either sign.
- Hansen, Lunde (2005). [A forecast comparison of volatility models: does anything beat a GARCH(1,1)?](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:jae:japmet:v:20:y:2005:i:7:p:873-889): 330 models: none clearly beat GARCH(1,1) on currency data, but on IBM shares models that treat falls differently did better.

## Fundamentals

- Piotroski (2000). [Value investing: the use of historical financial statement information](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:joares:v:38:y:2000:i::p:1-41): A nine-point accounting score separated stronger from weaker cheap US shares, 1976–1996, mainly in small, thinly followed firms.
- Bernard, Thomas (1989). [Post-earnings-announcement drift: delayed price response or risk premium?](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:joares:v:27:y:1989:i::p:1-36): Shares with good earnings surprises kept beating those with bad ones in most quarters from 1974 to 1985. The effect has weakened since.

## Macro

- Estrella, Mishkin (1996). [The yield curve as a predictor of US recessions](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/current_issues/ci2-7.pdf): The 10-year minus 3-month spread beat other indicators for predicting recessions two to six quarters ahead.
- Driesprong, Jacobsen, Maat (2008). [Striking oil: another puzzle?](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:89:y:2008:i:2:p:307-327): Oil price changes predicted stock returns in many countries, which the authors read as investors under-reacting. Results are in-sample.
- Miranda-Agrippino, Rey (2017 / 2021). [US monetary policy and the global financial cycle (review)](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP16580.pdf): US policy helps drive a global cycle in asset prices, credit and risk appetite.
- Caldara, Iacoviello (2022). [Measuring geopolitical risk](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20191823): A news-based index: higher geopolitical risk comes with weaker investment and jobs and falling shares. The share-price effect was short-lived in earlier drafts.
- Overview of the ICE US Dollar Index (n.d.). [U.S. Dollar Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Dollar_Index): Weights: euro 57.6%, yen 13.6%, pound 11.9%, Canadian dollar 9.1%, krona 4.2%, franc 3.6%. Changed only once, when the euro began.

## Market efficiency

- Fama (1970). [Efficient capital markets: a review of theory and empirical work](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/efficient-capital-markets-a-review-of-theory-and-empirical-work-fama-eugene/10005302862): The classic statement that prices reflect available information.
- Grossman, Stiglitz (1980). [On the impossibility of informationally efficient markets](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1130000794301884928?lang=en): If prices held all information nobody would pay to gather it, so markets can only be nearly efficient.

## Markets

- Bank for International Settlements (2025). [OTC foreign exchange turnover in April 2025 (Triennial Survey)](https://www.bis.org/statistics/rpfx25_fx.htm): $9.6 trillion a day; swaps 42%, spot 31%, forwards 19%, options 7%; USD on 89.2% of trades, EUR 28.9%, JPY 16.8%, GBP 10.2%.
- CME Group (n.d.). [Micro E-mini futures products overview](https://cmegroup.com/cn-t/education/courses/micro-e-mini-futures/micro-e-mini-futures-products-overview.html): E-mini S&P 500 has a $50 multiplier and $12.50 tick; the Micro has a $5 multiplier.
- CFTC (n.d.). [Commitments of Traders report descriptions and release schedule](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm): Weekly positions data, generally released Friday for the previous Tuesday. Reports suggest a twice-weekly schedule may follow; check the schedule page.
- FINRA (n.d.). [Duration: what an interest rate hike could do to your bond portfolio](https://www.finra.org/investors/alerts/duration-what-interest-rate-hike-could-do-your-bond-portfolio): Higher duration means more rate sensitivity: a 10-year-duration fund falls about 10% if rates rise 1%.
- SEC (n.d.). [Interest rate risk: when interest rates go up, prices of fixed-rate bonds fall](https://www.sec.gov/files/ib_interestraterisk.pdf): Investor bulletin on why bond prices move opposite to yields.
- FINRA and SEC (2009). [FINRA, SEC warn retail investors about leveraged or inverse ETFs](https://finra.org/media-center/news-releases/2009/finra-sec-warn-retail-investors-about-investing-leveraged-or-inverse): Most reset daily, so multi-day results can differ sharply from the stated multiple.
- National Law Review summary of the SEC order (2024). [SEC approves 11 bitcoin spot ETFs](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-approves-11-bitcoin-spot-etfs): SEC approved 11 spot bitcoin products on 10 January 2024.

## Momentum and trend

- Jegadeesh, Titman (1993). [Returns to buying winners and selling losers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_investing): Past 3–12 month stock winners kept beating losers over the next 3–12 months. Link is an overview page; the paper is Journal of Finance 48(1).
- Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen (2012). [Time series momentum](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463): Significant momentum in all 58 liquid futures tested across equity, currency, commodity and bond markets.
- Hurst, Ooi, Pedersen (2017). [A century of evidence on trend-following investing](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/hurst.pdf): Positive trend returns over more than 100 years and good results in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns. Written by a fund manager that runs such strategies.
- Daniel, Moskowitz (2016). [Momentum crashes](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:20439): Momentum can crash in rebounds after market falls when volatility is high.
- Szakmary, Shen, Sharma (2010). [Trend-following trading strategies in commodity futures: a re-examination](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:34:y:2010:i:2:p:409-426): Moving-average and channel rules on 28 commodity markets over 48 years, positive after costs in most markets. A later study of US stocks found such profits vanished after 2007.

## Options

- Callan Associates for Cboe (n.d.). [An analysis of index option writing](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Callan_CBOE.pdf): Commissioned by the exchange. Buy-write indexes showed better risk-adjusted results than stocks over 18 years, with their own crash exposure.
- Carr, Wu (2009). [Variance risk premiums](https://typeset.io/pdf/variance-risk-premiums-2r5d548tv2.pdf): Stock index options price in more variance than usually follows, in good and bad markets.
- Bakshi, Kapadia (2003). [Delta-hedged gains and the negative market volatility risk premium](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i2p527-566.html): S&P 500 option buyers who hedged away direction lost on average, and lost more when volatility was high.
- Coval, Shumway (2001). [Expected option returns](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:56:y:2001:i:3:p:983-1009): Zero-beta at-the-money S&P 500 straddles lost about 3% per week on average.
- Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (2021). [Hedging demand and market intraday momentum](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403): Over 60 futures, 1974–2020: hedging short gamma trades with the move, and links to intraday momentum in the last half hour.
- Driessen, Maenhout, Vilkov (2009). [The price of correlation risk: evidence from equity options](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:64:y:2009:i:3:p:1377-1406): Index options look expensive because they insure against rising correlation, but the premium could not be exploited with realistic trading frictions.
- Pan, Poteshman (2006). [The information in option volume for future stock prices](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:19:y:2006:i:3:p:871-908): Put-call ratios from non-public data on new buyer-initiated trades predicted stock returns over the next days. The public ratio is a different number.
- Rubinstein (1994). [Implied binomial trees](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb00079.x) (not re-opened): Classic paper on the volatility smile in index options after the 1987 crash. Cited from general knowledge and from summaries, not re-opened.

## Order flow

- Evans, Lyons (2002). [Order flow and exchange rate dynamics](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7317): Signed dealer order flow explains much of daily exchange rate moves, unlike macro models.
- Osler (2003). [Currency orders and exchange rate dynamics](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:58:y:2003:i:5:p:1791-1819): Bank order data: take-profit orders cluster at round numbers and stop-loss orders cluster just beyond them, which can explain some chart behaviour.
- Cont, Kukanov, Stoikov (2014). [The price impact of order book events](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402): In 50 US stocks, order flow imbalance at the best prices is the main driver of very short-term price changes, and the effect is stronger when the book is thin.
- Steidlmayer; Steidlmayer and Koy (overview page) (1986). [Market Profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile): Overview of the chart built at the Chicago Board of Trade in the 1980s: letters per half hour, point of control and a 70% value area. No reliable success rates exist.
- Aquilina, Budish, O'Neill (2022). [Quantifying the high-frequency trading arms race](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-no-50-quantifying-high-frequency-trading-arms-race-new-methodology): UK share data: speed races are about a fifth of volume, and a handful of firms take most of the wins.

## Other strategies

- Gatev, Goetzmann, Rouwenhorst (2006). [Pairs trading: performance of a relative-value arbitrage rule](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:19:y:2006:i:3:p:797-827): Daily US data 1962–2002: about 11% annual excess return for top pairs. Later studies find profits have fallen.
- Bouman, Jacobsen (2002). [The Halloween indicator: sell in May and go away](https://www.tias.edu/docs/default-source/Kennisartikelen/ssrn-id76248.pdf?sfvrsn=2): November–April stock returns beat May–October in 36 of 37 countries, 1970–1998. Later work disputes how strong this is.
- Lucca, Moench (2015). [The pre-FOMC announcement drift](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr512.pdf): US stocks gained unusually in the day before Fed meetings; a later update found it mostly at meetings with a press conference.

## Prop firms

- Finance Magnates (2025). [My Forex Funds wins dismissal of CFTC case](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/myforexfunds-hints-at-comeback-after-winning-legal-battle-against-cftc/): CFTC sued in 2023; the case was dismissed in 2025 and left the wider questions open.
- Track360 (industry source, unaudited) (2026). [Prop trading industry statistics](https://track360.io/blog/prop-trading-industry-statistics-2026): Roughly 12–14% of challenges pass and about 7% of buyers are ever paid. Treat as a rough, industry-reported estimate.

## Psychology

- Odean (1998). [Are investors reluctant to realize their losses?](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/Papers%20current%20versions/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf): Linked review: investors sell winners more readily than losers.
- Kahneman, Tversky (1979). [Prospect theory: an analysis of decision under risk](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185) (not re-opened): Losses are felt more strongly than equal gains. Not re-opened in this research pass.

## Regulation

- SEC / FINRA (2026). [Federal Register notice approving amendments to FINRA Rule 4210](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-17/html/2026-07485.htm): Removes the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum; FINRA Notice 26-10 gives 4 June 2026 as effective. Brokers may roll this out over time, so check yours.
- CFTC and NASAA (n.d.). [Investor alert: foreign exchange currency fraud](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/cftcnasaaforexalert.html): Regulators warn off-exchange retail forex is at best very risky and can be fraud; check a firm's registration first.
- FINRA (2022). [Regulatory Notice 22-08: complex products and options](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/FINRA-Regulatory-Notice-22-08-Complex-Products-and-Options-5.9.2022.pdf): Firms reminded that options can be risky for retail investors who do not understand them.
- Pinsent Masons, Hadef & Partners, Clyde & Co (2025–26). [UAE licensing regime for finfluencers (SCA Chairman Resolution No. 10 of 2025)](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uae-new-licensing-regime-finfluencers): Licensing for people giving financial recommendations on social media. Law-firm summaries, not the text of the resolution; the regulator is reported renamed CMA.

## Retail outcomes

- European Securities and Markets Authority (2018). [ESMA agrees to prohibit binary options and restrict CFDs to protect retail investors](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933): National regulators found 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money; average losses per client €1,600 to €29,000. Sets leverage caps from 30:1 down to 2:1.
- Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean (2014). [The cross-section of speculator skill: evidence from day trading](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24): Taiwan day traders 1992–2006: under 1% predictably earn positive returns after fees.
- Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti (2020). [Day trading for a living?](https://www.scribd.com/document/428540384/SSRN-id3423101): Brazilian futures day traders who persisted: about 3% profited after fees. The page seen is a copy of the working paper, so check the journal version before quoting it.
- Barber, Odean (2000). [Trading is hazardous to your wealth](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:55:y:2000:i:2:p:773-806): 66,465 US households 1991–96: those trading most earned 11.4% a year, the market 17.9%.
- Bryzgalova, Pavlova, Sikorskaya (2023). [Retail trading in options and the rise of the big three wholesalers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:17688): Retail option buyers favour cheap weekly options with wide spreads and lose on average.
- de Silva, Smith, So (2022). [Losing is optional: retail option trading and earnings announcement volatility](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/losing-is-optional-retail-option-trading-and-earnings-announcement-volatility-silva-tim/10013403980): Retail buyers overpay around earnings, pay large spreads, and react slowly.

## Risk

- Kelly (overview page) (1956). [Kelly criterion](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion): Maximises long-run growth when the odds are known; markets do not give known odds.
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2016). [Minimum capital requirements for market risk](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d352.htm): Moves bank market-risk rules from value-at-risk to expected shortfall under stress. Later superseded by a January 2019 version.
- Artzner, Delbaen, Eber, Heath (1999). [Coherent measures of risk](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:mathfi:v:9:y:1999:i:3:p:203-228): Sets out what a good risk measure should do, and shows that quantile measures such as value-at-risk can fail to reward diversification.
- Pérignon, Smith (2010). [The level and quality of value-at-risk disclosure by commercial banks](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:34:y:2010:i:2:p:362-377): Historical simulation is by far the most popular bank method, and it contained very little information about future volatility.
- DeMiguel, Garlappi, Uppal (2009). [Optimal versus naive diversification](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:5:p:1915-1953): Across 14 models and seven datasets, none reliably beat simply holding equal weights.
- Frazzini, Pedersen (2014). [Betting against beta](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:111:y:2014:i:1:p:1-25): High-beta assets earned less than theory predicts across many markets. A later paper (Novy-Marx and Velikov, 2022) disputes how the strategy is built.
- Kaminski, Lo (2014). [When do stop-loss rules stop losses?](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp234-254.html): In a random market stops only lower return. With momentum they can add return and cut volatility.

## Sentiment

- Tetlock (2007). [Giving content to investor sentiment: the role of media in the stock market](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:62:y:2007:i:3:p:1139-1168): Pessimistic Wall Street Journal text predicted falling prices that later reversed.
- Baker, Wurgler (2006). [Investor sentiment and the cross-section of stock returns](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:61:y:2006:i:4:p:1645-1680): When sentiment is high, small, young and hard-to-value shares later earn relatively low returns.
- NerdWallet explainer of the CNN index (n.d.). [What is the Fear and Greed Index?](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/investing/what-is-the-fear-and-greed-index): Seven equally weighted market measures on a 0 to 100 scale. A secondary description.
- Rapach, Ringgenberg, Zhou (2016). [Short interest and aggregate stock returns](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:121:y:2016:i:1:p:46-65): Total US short interest was a strong predictor of later market returns, in and out of sample, which suggests short sellers are often well informed.
- Frazzini, Lamont (2008). [Dumb money: mutual fund flows and the cross-section of stock returns](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:88:y:2008:i:2:p:299-322): Retail money moving into funds tends to precede lower returns for the favoured shares.
- Cohen, Malloy, Pomorski (2012). [Decoding inside information](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:67:y:2012:i:3:p:1009-1043): Unusual insider trades carried all the predictive power, about 0.8% a month in the working paper, and routine trades carried none.

## Technical analysis

- Brock, Lakonishok, LeBaron (1992). [Simple technical trading rules and the stochastic properties of stock returns](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:47:y:1992:i:5:p:1731-64): Moving-average and range-break rules on the Dow 1897–1986 looked informative. Later work disputes how much survives costs and data snooping.
- Sullivan, Timmermann, White (1999). [Data-snooping, technical trading rule performance, and the bootstrap](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Sullivan_Timmermann_White.pdf): Tests a large universe of rules on 100 years of Dow data, correcting for having searched through so many.
- Park, Irwin (2007). [What do we know about the profitability of technical analysis?](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jecsur:v:21:y:2007:i:4:p:786-826): Positive results are common but most studies have problems such as data snooping and choosing rules after the fact.
- Lo, Mamaysky, Wang (2000). [Foundations of technical analysis](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:55:y:2000:i:4:p:1705-1765): Computer-detected chart patterns carried some extra information; that is not the same as a profit after costs.
- Marshall, Young, Rose (2006). [Candlestick technical trading strategies: can they create value for investors?](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v26y2014icp65-78.html): The study behind later tests. Page linked is a follow-up on Taiwan, which found results depend heavily on the exit rule.
- SAGE Open study (2017). [Profitability of candlestick charting patterns in the Stock Exchange of Thailand](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244017736799): Mean returns of most reversal patterns were not statistically different from zero.
- Batchelor, Ramyar (2006). [Magic numbers in the Dow](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16276/): Tested Fibonacci and round-fraction clustering in Dow trends: a few significant ratios, no more than chance would produce.
- Menkhoff, Taylor (2007). [The obstinate passion of foreign exchange professionals: technical analysis](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:jeclit:v:45:y:2007:i:4:p:936-972): Survey of why FX professionals keep using technical analysis.
- Neely, Weller, Ulrich (2009). [The adaptive markets hypothesis: evidence from the foreign exchange market](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v44y2009i02p467-488_09.html): Profits to particular FX rules fade over time as more traders learn them.
- Brown, Goetzmann, Kumar (1998). [The Dow Theory: William Peter Hamilton's track record reconsidered](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/26858): Re-tests the Wall Street Journal editor's calls of 1902–1929 with risk adjustment and finds useful timing, reversing an early 1934 study. Later on, trading frictions remove most of the benefit.
- Fang, Jacobsen, Qin (2014). [Predictability of the simple technical trading rules: an out-of-sample test](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/predictability-simple-technical-trading-rules-855670296): Tests well-known technical rules on fresh data from 1987 to 2011 and finds no evidence that they predict stock markets over that period.
- Wilder (1978). [New concepts in technical trading systems (ADX overview page)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_directional_movement_index) (not re-opened): The book that introduced RSI, ATR, ADX and Parabolic SAR. Cited from general knowledge.

## Testing

- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu (2014). [The probability of backtest overfitting](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253): Great-looking backtests are easy to reach after trying only a modest number of variations.
- Harvey, Liu, Zhu (2016). [...and the cross-section of expected returns](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:20592): With hundreds of tested factors, a new finding needs a t-statistic above about 3, not 2.
- McLean, Pontiff (2016). [Does academic research destroy stock return predictability?](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/does-academic-research-destroy-855658380): 97 published predictors shrink out of sample and shrink more after publication. The 26%/58% decline figures were seen only in secondary tables.

## Who uses what

- Taylor, Allen (Bank of England) (1989 / 1992). [Chart analysis and the foreign exchange market](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1989/chart-analysis-and-the-foreign-exchange-market): Survey of London currency dealers: most put some weight on charts for short horizons, and less for long ones. It is old and covers one market.
- Hoffmann, Shefrin (2014). [Technical analysis and individual investors](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jeborg:v:107:y:2014:i:pb:p:487-511): Clients of one Dutch online broker: about a third reported using technical analysis, and those who did tended to do worse, above all heavy options traders. One sample only.
- Pinto, Robinson, Stowe (2019). [Equity valuation: a survey of professional practice](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:revfec:v:37:y:2019:i:2:p:219-233): Survey of investment professionals: market multiples and present-value models dominate. It shows what people use, not which method is accurate.
- BarclayHedge (2018). [Majority of hedge fund pros use AI/machine learning in investment strategies](https://barclayhedge.com/research/press_releases/PR_July_18_2018-survey.html): A short industry poll with only 55 replies, so treat it as a rough signal of direction.
